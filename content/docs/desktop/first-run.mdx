When APM finds no vault, it opens on **Welcome to APM**. You have three choices:

- **Create a new vault.** Set a master password and pick how hard the key is to crack.
- **Open an existing vault.** Choose a `vault.dat` made by `pm` or another computer.
- **Restore from cloud.** Download your vault from Google Drive, GitHub or Dropbox.

If you already use `pm`, the app usually finds your vault on its own and opens straight to the lock screen. See [where the app looks](/docs/desktop#the-vault-file).

The footer's **Documentation** link opens these docs in your browser.

## Create a new vault

The setup has seven steps, listed on the left, and the top of each says **Step N of 7**. The last two are optional. **Back** returns to the previous step until the vault is created, and **Cancel** on the first step returns to the Welcome screen. After the vault is created, the first five steps are locked. Under the steps, the app notes **Everything stays on this Mac unless you turn on sync.**

The vault is created at the path shown under **Vault file** in step 5, with the name **Personal vault**. Rename it later in **Settings → General → Vault**.

<Steps>

### Password

Type your master password twice under **Master password** and **Confirm master password**. It must meet every rule in the checklist:

- At least 8 characters
- An uppercase letter
- A lowercase letter
- A number
- A symbol from `!@#$%^&*()-_=+`

**Continue** stays disabled until every rule is ticked and both fields match. Until they match, the second field says **The passwords do not match.**

A strength meter shows the estimated time to crack it offline, such as **cracked in 4 days offline** or **outlasts the universe offline**. It assumes an attacker who tries 10 billion guesses a second. **Suggest** fills both fields with four capitalized words joined by hyphens, a digit on one of the words and a closing `!`, such as `Harbor-Lantern7-Velvet-Comet!`. Write it down before you continue.

<Callout type="warning" title="APM cannot reset this password">
The password never leaves this computer, and APM has no way to reset it. Set up recovery in step 6, or later in **Settings → Recovery**, so you have a way back in.
</Callout>

### Security

Choose how expensive each guess at your password is. A line such as **This Mac has 10 cores and 8 GB of memory, so APM recommends Hardened.** shows what the app detected, and the profile it recommends is marked **Recommended**: **Paranoid** with 16 GB and 8 cores or more, **Hardened** with 8 GB and 4 cores or more, **Standard** otherwise. The app reads these numbers from its window rather than from the system, so its pick can differ from the one `pm setup` makes.

| Profile | Key derivation | Cipher | Unlock time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | Argon2id, t=3, 64 MiB, p=2 | AES-256-GCM | about 0.3s |
| **Hardened** | Argon2id, t=5, 256 MiB, p=4 | AES-256-GCM | about 1s |
| **Paranoid** | Argon2id, t=6, 512 MiB, p=4 | XChaCha20-Poly1305 | about 2s |
| **Custom** | Your own settings | Your choice | Depends |

Each profile shows its settings and unlock time, for example **Argon2id · t=5 · 256 MiB · p=4 · unlock about 1s**.

With **Custom**, set **Memory** (32 to 1024 MiB, in steps of 32), **Time cost** (1 to 10), **Threads** (1 to 16), **Salt length** (16, 24, 32 or 64 bytes) and **Nonce length** (12 or 24 bytes). It starts at 128 MiB, t=4, 4 threads, a 32-byte salt and a 12-byte nonce. The **Legacy** profile is not offered here; only `pm` can set it.

Under **Cipher**, pick **AES-256-GCM** (hardware accelerated on Apple silicon and modern x86) or **XChaCha20-Poly1305** (constant-time in software, 24-byte nonce). Picking a profile resets the cipher to that profile's default, so choose the cipher last. XChaCha20-Poly1305 always uses a 24-byte nonce, whatever **Nonce length** says. You can change the profile and cipher later. See [encryption profiles](/docs/security/profiles).

### Spaces

Spaces keep groups of secrets apart inside one vault. Every vault has a **Default** space. Setup starts you with **Personal** and **Work**. Remove one with its `×`, type a name and press <Kbd>↵</Kbd> or choose **Add**, or click a suggestion (**Family**, **Homelab**, **Clients**, **Finance**). A name you already added, in any capitalization, is not added twice, and `Default` is skipped because every vault has it. Each new space gets the next of seven colors. See [Spaces](/docs/desktop/spaces).

### Unlock

- **Unlock with Touch ID** (macOS) stores your master password in your macOS login keychain. APM asks for your fingerprint before it reads it. See [Touch ID](/docs/desktop/lock-and-unlock#touch-id). It starts on when this Mac can use Touch ID, and is off and disabled when it cannot.
- **Session length**: how long an unlock lasts, even while you are active (**15 minutes**, **1 hour**, **4 hours** or **12 hours**). The default is **1 hour**.
- **Lock when idle**: lock after this long without activity (**1 minute**, **5 minutes**, **15 minutes** or **30 minutes**). The default is **15 minutes**.

APM also locks when your computer sleeps or the screen locks. Change Touch ID later in **Settings → Security**, and the lock rules in **Settings → Sessions**.

### Create

Review the **Vault file** path, **Profile**, **Cipher**, **Spaces**, **Touch ID** and **Lock** (for example **After 15 min idle, 1 h max**). Choose **Create vault**. A progress bar shows **Deriving your key** while the vault is encrypted for the first time, then **Vault created**. The bar is an estimate, so it may pause near the end on a slow computer. **Create vault** then becomes **Continue**.

APM also creates an empty `policies` folder next to the vault, for [password policies](/docs/reference/policies).

If something goes wrong here, the message shows under the review:

| Message | What to do |
|---|---|
| **A vault already exists at** the path | The app is pointed at a file that already holds a vault, for example after **Open another vault**. APM never overwrites it. Go back to the first step, choose **Cancel**, then **Back to** and your vault's name. To keep a second vault, create it with `pm setup --vault` and a new path, then open it with **Open an existing vault** |
| A password rule, such as **password must be at least 8 characters long** | The engine checked the password again. Go back to step 1 |

If APM cannot store the password for Touch ID, the vault is still created, with Touch ID off and no message. Turn it on later in **Settings → Security**.

### Recovery (optional)

Enter a **Recovery email** and choose **Send code**. APM emails a 6-digit code, with a subject such as **123456 - APM Email Verification**, and shows "APM sent a 6-digit code to you@example.com. It expires in 15 minutes." Type the code into the boxes, or paste it. The button becomes **Resend** if you need a new code. As soon as the code is accepted, APM creates your recovery key and shows it.

If the code is wrong, more than 15 minutes old, or the fifth wrong try, you see **That code does not match. Check the email and try again.** Choose **Resend** and use the newest code. If the email could not be sent, you see **Could not reach the provider. Check your connection and try again.**

Your recovery key appears once, under **Email verified**, in the form `K7M3-P2R9-WXHT-4826`. Then:

- **Copy** puts it on the clipboard, and APM clears the clipboard after 60 seconds if it still holds the key.
- **Save as file** asks where to save `APM-recovery-key.txt`, a text file with the key, the vault's name and today's date.
- Tick **I stored this key somewhere safe**. **Continue** stays disabled until you do. APM keeps only a wrapped copy that the key unlocks.

APM also emails **SECURITY ALERT: APM Vault Recovery Configured** to the address. That email never contains the key.

A code sent to your email plus this key can reset your master password. Choose **Skip** to set it up later in **Settings → Recovery**. If you sent a code but don't want to finish, **Skip** is the way on: **Continue** waits for a verified code. See [Recovery](/docs/desktop/recovery).

### Sync (optional)

Choose where to keep an encrypted copy:

- **Keep it on this Mac**: no network. Choose **Finish**.
- **GitHub**: enter a **Repository** (`owner/repo`, private, one you own) and a **Personal access token** with Contents read and write on it. **Connect and finish** stays disabled until the repository looks like `owner/repo` and the token is longer than eight characters.
- **Google Drive**: your own Drive, through a browser sign-in. The step says APM asks for access to a single app folder. In fact Google asks you to let APM see the files it creates, and the vault is uploaded to the top of your My Drive, under a random name such as `v_3fa91c.bin`.
- **Dropbox**: your own Dropbox app, through a browser sign-in. That needs a Dropbox app key and secret, which this step does not ask for, so connecting here stops with **Self-hosted Dropbox needs your app key and app secret.** Choose **Skip** and connect Dropbox in **Settings → Sync** instead, where you can enter them.

Choose **Connect and finish**. For Google Drive your browser opens to sign in. Providers only ever receive the encrypted file. To use APM's shared storage with a retrieval key, connect later in **Settings → Sync**. See [Cloud sync](/docs/desktop/sync).

If the provider rejects the connection or cannot be reached, the message is **Could not reach the provider. Check your connection and try again.**, whatever the provider said. Check the repository and the token, or choose **Skip** and connect later in **Settings → Sync**.

</Steps>

### Your vault is ready

The last screen confirms the encryption settings, for example **Encrypted with Argon2id · t=5 · 256 MiB · p=4 and AES-256-GCM.**, and suggests what to do next:

- **Import passwords** opens the [import](/docs/desktop/import-export) flow.
- **Add your first item** opens the **New item** picker.
- **Connect the browser extension** opens **Settings → Browser extension**.

Or choose **Open vault** to go straight in.

## Open an existing vault

Choose **Open an existing vault** on the Welcome screen, or **Open another vault** on the lock screen. The **Open a vault** screen shows the **Current file** and its **State**: **Vault found** or **No vault at this path yet**.

- **Choose vault file** opens a file picker for a `.dat` or `.vault` file. Switch the picker to **All files** to choose a file with another name. APM switches to it, and remembers it for next time.
- **Use the default location** switches to the path the app would use with no choice saved: `APM_VAULT_PATH`, a `vault.dat` next to the `pm` on your `PATH`, `~/Desktop/apm/vault.dat`, then `~/.apm/vault.dat`. If no vault is there, the Welcome screen follows, so you can create one.
- **Back** returns to where you were.

If you came from another vault's lock screen, the app returns to the Welcome screen after the switch. Choose **Back to** and the vault's name to reach its lock screen.

<Callout type="warning" title="Pick a real vault file">
APM does not check what is inside the file you pick. If you choose the wrong file, the lock screen still appears, and every unlock attempt counts as a wrong password toward the [failed-attempts limit](/docs/desktop/lock-and-unlock#failed-attempts). Open the right file before you try your password.
</Callout>

APM works on one `vault.dat` at a time. To point `pm` at the same file, set `APM_VAULT_PATH` or pass `--vault`. **Settings → Developer** can copy the `export` line for your shell. See [The app and pm](/docs/desktop/terminal).

<Callout type="note">
Switching files locks the vault and restarts the background engine on the new file. The lock screen then asks for that vault's master password.
</Callout>

## Restore from cloud

Use this on a new computer to download a vault you synced from another one.

<Steps>

### Find your vault

Choose a **Provider**:

- **GitHub**: enter the **Repository** (`owner/repo`) and a **Personal access token** that can read it. The token is saved in the restored vault, so sync to that repository keeps working.
- **Google Drive** or **Dropbox**: enter the **Retrieval key** shown when you set up sync. `pm cloud init` prints it too. The app looks the key up in APM's public storage, so this only finds a vault you synced with **APM public**. For a vault in your own Drive or Dropbox app, restore it with `pm cloud get` in a terminal instead.

Choose **Continue**. An empty field stops you with **Enter the repository as owner/repo.**, **Enter a token that can read the repository.** or **Enter the retrieval key.**

If a vault already exists on this computer, a **Save the restored vault** dialog asks where to put the restored copy, with `vault-restored.dat` suggested. APM switches to that file. It never overwrites the vault you have. If you pick a file that already exists and confirm, APM first copies that file to the same name plus `.backup.` and the date and time, such as `vault-restored.dat.backup.20261005-141502`.

### Unlock it

The screen says **Ready to download from** the repository, **Google Drive** or **Dropbox**, and **Saved to** the path **once it decrypts.** Enter the master password the vault was created with and choose **Download and unlock**. APM downloads the sealed file, checks the password, saves the file and opens the vault.

</Steps>

| Message | What it means |
|---|---|
| **That is not the master password for the downloaded vault.** | The download worked, but the password does not open it. Try again. Nothing is saved until it works |
| **No vault matches that key:** followed by the provider's error | The retrieval key is wrong, or the vault was not synced with APM public. You go back to the first step |
| **Could not reach the provider. Check your connection and try again.** | The download failed, for any reason. For GitHub, also check the repository and the token |

<Callout type="note" title="If you cancel after choosing where to save">
APM switches to the new file as soon as you choose **Download and unlock**. If the restore then fails and you leave, the app is still pointed at that empty path and shows the Welcome screen. Your old vault is untouched: choose **Open an existing vault** and pick it again.
</Callout>

## Go back to your vault

When a vault exists, the Welcome screen shows **Back to** and your vault's name. Click it to return to the lock screen without changing anything.
